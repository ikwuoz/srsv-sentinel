00 · THE SEAT

What does a charter do?

Short answer — holding one makes you a banker: your branches earn a pro-rata share of every epoch’s issuance. 1,000 Founding Charters at genesis (soulbound), then daily Dutch auctions in ETH.

Lifecycle with teeth: retire your last branch and the charter burns — no revolving doors. The only way back in is winning another auction.

01 · YIELD

One branch, what is it worth?

Short answer — a branch’s daily take is base × m ÷ N: base issuance times the policy multiplier, split across all branches. More branches = bigger cut, but every new branch dilutes everyone.

04 · EXITS

What does it cost to leave?

Short answer — retiring a branch mints its share minus a congestion-priced resolution fee: quadratic from a floor (quiet) to a ceiling (bank run) on 7-day exit pressure. Half burns, half pays those who stayed.

06 · DEFENSE

How fast can the bank buy back?

Short answer — in contraction the vault burns everything it buys, but rate-limited: min(10% of vault, 0.2% of pool) per hourly tick — so defense can't be baited into one blockable shot. Unspent balance rolls forward; the vault never sells.

07 · SUPPLY

When does the budget run out?

Short answer — 1B hard cap, 100M genesis liquidity, 900M issuance budget. Circulating supply is a receipt: withdrawals − burns. Burns (licenses, buybacks, half of exit fees) don't just cut float — they permanently lower the maximum supply that can ever exist, and stretch the runway below.
